We study the preflop stage of six-player no-limit Texas hold'em cash games at $100$ big blinds as a finite game in its own right, and compute approximate equilibria for it. The game has one raise size per level, cold calls and squeezes, and rake; its \num{7499} decision nodes end either in a fold, a called all-in, or the flop. At the flop we stop and pay each player a share of the pot given by their equity scaled by a \emph{realization factor}, a parametric model of how well a hand converts equity into winnings after the flop, built from suitedness, connectivity (including wheel straights), pairs, position, initiative, domination and pot depth.

We solve the game with external-sampling Monte Carlo counterfactual regret minimisation with linear averaging, which solves the full tree in about a minute on a laptop. On a heads-up push/fold game, where convergence is guaranteed, exploitability falls to $1.5\times 10^{-5}$ big blinds per hand. The realization parameters are fitted to published equilibrium charts by coordinate search over full solves; the solution's category-balanced distance to the charts is $0.136$, against $0.319$ for a position-only model. A solve with the charts locked shows that the remaining disagreement is concentrated in how often offsuit aces and broadways re-raise. We also study a cheaper fit that holds strategies fixed and treats the charts as quantal responses: it improves the frequent spots but cannot see equilibrium feedback in 4-bet pots, and moving halfway towards it gives a small, consistent gain. An interactive trainer grades decisions against the solution with importance-sampled rollouts.
