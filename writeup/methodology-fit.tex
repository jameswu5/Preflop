\section{A fixed-strategy fit}\label{sec:fixed-fit}

Coordinate search (\cref{sec:tuning}) pays a full solve for every candidate $\theta$, which caps the number of
realization parameters that can be tuned. The fixed-strategy fit trades that cost for an approximation: it holds every
player's strategy fixed and asks only how the action values at the charted spots move with $\theta$. The strategy held
fixed is the Anchored solve (\cref{sec:two-solves}), which plays the charts exactly where they exist and the solved
strategy elsewhere. Under it, the quantal-response loss of \cref{sec:fitting-maths} becomes a cheap, smooth function of
$\theta$. \Cref{fig:fit-flow} shows the pipeline.

\paragraph{The sampler.}
For every charted spot $s$, every hand class $k$ that the strategy brings to $s$, and every legal action $a$, the
sampler estimates $\mathrm{EV}(s,k,a)$ like the trainer's rollouts (\cref{sec:snis}). It draws the hero's combination
uniformly from class $k$ and the opponents' hands from the remaining fifty cards, weights the deal by the likelihood
$\ell$ of the opponents' actions on the way to $s$, and plays every action out on that deal, with everyone, the hero
included, following the fixed strategy afterwards. Equities are computed once per deal and live set, and shared by the
actions. Deals continue until the effective sample size reaches a target or a cap. A playout ends in one of three
ways. Folds and pots won without a flop do not depend on $\theta$, and nor do all-ins, which pay raw equity
(\cref{sec:shares-maths}). Only flop terminals do. The sampler therefore sums the first two kinds into a constant
$F_{s,k,a}$, and keeps every flop terminal as a \emph{record} $r$: its terminal $z_r$, the deal's weight $\ell_r$, and
each live player's class $k_{r,j}$ and equity $\eq_{r,j}$. With $W_{s,k} = \sum_n \ell_n$ over the deals and $h$ the
hero,
\begin{gather}
  \mathrm{EV}_\theta(s,k,a) = \frac{1}{W_{s,k}}\Big(F_{s,k,a} + \sum_{r \in \mathcal R_{s,k,a}} \ell_r\, \mathrm{pay}_\theta(r)\Big),
  \label{eq:fit-ev}\\
  \mathrm{pay}_\theta(r) = \big(\pot - \rake\big)(z_r)\, \frac{\real_h\, \eq_{r,h}}{\sum_{j} \real_j\, \eq_{r,j}} - C_h(z_r),
  \nonumber
\end{gather}
the sum running over the live players at $z_r$. This is \eqref{eq:snis} with the flop terminals left open. Because the
deals, boards and equities are fixed once sampled, \eqref{eq:fit-ev} is a deterministic, almost everywhere smooth
function of $\theta$: every candidate is evaluated on the same sample, a sample-average approximation with common random
numbers across $\theta$.

\paragraph{The fitter.}
A realization factor depends on a record only through the player's class and their \emph{role} at $z_r$: whether
they act last after the flop, whether they made the last raise, whether the pot was re-raised, and the stack-to-pot
ratio $\spr$. Far fewer roles than records occur, so $\real$ and its derivatives in $\theta$ are
evaluated on a role-by-class table, which the records index into. The loss is $\mathcal L(\theta)$ of
\cref{sec:fitting-maths}, with $\omega_{s,k}$ the class weights of \cref{def:tv-distance} balanced by category,
plus $\nu \sum_m \big((\theta_m - \theta_{0,m})/\delta_m\big)^2$, where $\theta_0$ is the sampling point and $\delta_m$
the coordinate-search step of parameter $m$. Its gradient is computed exactly in reverse order. The softmax gives
$\partial\mathcal L/\partial \mathrm{EV}$, \eqref{eq:fit-ev} gives $\partial \mathrm{EV}/\partial\,\mathrm{pay}_\theta(r)
= \ell_r/W_{s,k}$, and with $s_h$ the hero's share at $z_r$,
\[
  \frac{\partial\, \mathrm{pay}_\theta(r)}{\partial \real_j} = \big(\pot - \rake\big)(z_r)\,
  \frac{\eq_{r,j}\,\big(\1[j = h] - s_h\big)}{\sum_{m} \real_m\, \eq_{r,m}} .
\]
These are accumulated into the table and multiplied by the analytic $\partial\real/\partial\theta$, which is zero where
the floor of the realization model binds (\cref{sec:realization}). The optimiser is L-BFGS-B~\cite{byrd1995} with box
bounds, in units of the steps $\delta_m$. Records are sorted by $(s,k,a)$ and split into chunks that threads process in
parallel. A \emph{hold-out} option leaves out the spots whose name matches a pattern and reports them separately, as a
check that the fit generalises. The output is $\hat\theta$, the loss and the chart's regret by category before and
after the fit.

\paragraph{Checks.}
Three checks guard the pipeline. First, at the sampling point $\theta_0$ the fitter recomputes every record's payoff
and must reproduce the sampler's to floating-point precision, which validates the port of the realization model.
Second, the sampler's estimates \eqref{eq:fit-ev} at $\theta_0$ must agree with the trainer's independent rollouts at a
few spots within their standard errors (the first gate of the study). Third, a self-test writes a synthetic sample in
the same format, sets each chart to $\softmax(\mathrm{EV}_{\theta^*}/\tau)$ for a planted $\theta^*$ away from the
sampling point, and checks that the fit recovers $\theta^*$ to a small fraction of a step. It also checks the analytic
gradient against finite differences.

\paragraph{What the fit cannot see.}
The fit asks whether the charts are quantal best responses to opponents who keep playing the fixed strategy. A full
re-solve under $\hat\theta$ also moves the other players' ranges, most of all in 4-bet pots where the ranges are
narrow, and the fit is blind to that. We therefore treat $\hat\theta$ as a direction, score points along the segment
from $\theta_0$ towards it with full solves and the distance of \cref{def:tv-distance}, and adopt only what those
confirm (\cref{sec:res-fit}).
